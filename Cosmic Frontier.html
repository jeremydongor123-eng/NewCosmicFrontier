Explore the universe

Planets

Worlds orbiting stars. Our solar system has eight recognized planets, from rocky Mercury to ice giant Neptune.

Key fact: Jupiter's mass is more than twice that of all the other planets combined.

Galaxies

Vast gravitational systems containing stars, gas, dust, stellar remnants, and dark matter.

Key fact: The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy; our Sun lies in one of its spiral arms.

Black Holes

Regions whose event horizons mark the boundary beyond which escape is impossible.

Key fact: The first image of a black hole's shadow (M87*) was released in 2019 by the Event Horizon Telescope.

Nebulae

Interstellar clouds of gas and dust. Some are stellar nurseries; others are remnants of dying stars.

Key fact: The Orion Nebula is visible to the naked eye as the fuzzy middle "star" of Orion's sword.

Exoplanets

Planets beyond our solar system, detected using methods such as transits and radial velocity.

Key fact: The first exoplanet found around a Sun-like star, 51 Pegasi b, was announced in 1995.

Neutron Stars

Ultra-dense stellar remnants. Some rotate rapidly and emit beams observed as pulsars.

Key fact: A teaspoon of neutron-star material would weigh billions of tonnes.

Stellar evolution

The Life of a Star

Every star is a long-running balance between gravity and the energy released by nuclear fusion.

How stars work

Stars form when dense regions of molecular clouds collapse under gravity. As the core heats and compresses, hydrogen fusion can begin. The star then spends much of its life on the main sequence.

    Stage 3 of 6

    Main sequence

    Hydrogen fusion in the core supplies energy that counteracts gravitational collapse.

    Stages at a glance

    Stage 1

    Nebula

    Cold gas and dust gather into dense regions that may collapse to form stars.

    Stage 2

    Protostar

    A forming star heats as gravity compresses its material. Fusion has not yet stabilized.

    Stage 3

    Main Sequence

    Hydrogen fusion in the core supplies energy that counteracts gravitational collapse.

    Stage 4

    Giant phase

    After core hydrogen is depleted, a star's structure changes. The details depend on its mass.

    Possible ending

    White Dwarf

    Many low- and intermediate-mass stars leave behind a hot, dense stellar core.

    Possible ending

    Supernova remnant

    Massive stars may explode as supernovae, leaving neutron stars or black holes.

    Fast facts about stars

    • Star colour is related to surface temperature: blue stars are generally hotter than red stars.
    • The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old.
    • A star's mass is a major factor controlling its lifetime and final fate.
    • The elements in planets and living organisms were forged through earlier generations of stars and stellar explosions.

    NASA Artemis III

    NASA currently describes Artemis III as a crewed low-Earth-orbit demonstration to test rendezvous and docking with commercial lunar lander systems before a planned 2028 landing mission.

    Recent discoveries and research

    Checked against official NASA, ESA, ESO, and Space Telescope Science Institute releases available on 24 November 2026.

    12 November 2026 · NASA

    Hubble gallery showcases gravitational lenses

    A NASA feature presents a gallery of gravitational lenses observed by Hubble. These warped and magnified views help astronomers study distant objects and prepare follow-up science with the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope.

    NASA research updates ↗
    10 November 2026 · NASA

    Could gravitational waves reveal new worlds?

    Two NASA-led studies describe how the future LISA gravitational-wave observatory could help reveal a population of exoplanets through the effects of orbiting systems on gravitational-wave signals. This is a proposed scientific capability, not a confirmed planet discovery.

    NASA source ↗
    2 November 2026 · NASA

    Hubble investigates missing oxygen in dwarf galaxies

    Observations of 37 nearby dwarf galaxies found less oxygen than researchers expected. The result raises questions about how gas and heavy elements move into and out of small galaxies.

    NASA source ↗
    15 September 2026 · Webb

    Webb reveals a dynamic star-forming region

    New observations of IC 348 show newborn stars shaping their surroundings and identify very low-mass brown dwarfs, including objects with masses around twice Jupiter's. The findings add clues about how stars and planetary-mass objects form.

    STScI release ↗
    3 September 2026 · ESO

    MOONS instrument achieves first light

    The new MOONS spectrograph at ESO's Very Large Telescope made its first observations. It is designed to observe up to 1,000 objects at once, helping researchers study stars and galaxies using visible and infrared light.

    ESO announcement ↗
    11 August 2026 · ESA/Webb

    Dust and water observed near the Milky Way's central black hole

    Webb observations indicate that dust and water can persist close to Sagittarius A*. The evolved star IRS 3, located about 0.55 light-years away, appears to contribute material despite the extreme environment.

    ESA/Webb release ↗

    How to read research news

    A press release is a summary, not the full scientific paper. Check the publication, methods, sample size, uncertainty, and whether a result is a direct observation, an interpretation, or a future possibility.

    Upcoming milestones and long-range goals

    Target dateLong-term goalResearch concept
    2027

    Artemis III

    Planned crewed demonstration in low Earth orbit to test key operations with commercial human landing system test vehicles.

    NASA lists a 2027 target for its infrared asteroid-hunting observatory. Launch timing remains subject to mission readiness.

    Dragonfly is planned to launch toward Titan, where it will investigate surface chemistry and habitability-related questions.

    Artemis IV target

    NASA currently targets this mission for a crewed lunar landing, following Artemis III systems demonstrations.

    Long term

    Moon to Mars

    Human exploration concepts include sustained lunar operations, improved life-support systems, radiation protection, and eventual crewed Mars missions. These are long-range goals, not fixed launch dates.

    Important distinction

    “Planned,” “targeted,” “under development,” and “proposed concept” mean different things. A concept such as interstellar laser-sail travel is not equivalent to a funded spacecraft with a scheduled launch.
